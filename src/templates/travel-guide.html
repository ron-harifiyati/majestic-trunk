01 · When to visit

Start with what you want to see.

The river changes the view—and which activities can run. There isn’t one perfect season for every trip.

Higher water

Power, mist and spray.

A fuller waterfall brings heavy spray that can soak the paths and obscure parts of the view. Bring rain protection and a dry bag for cameras or phones.

Some river activities change route or pause when levels are unsuitable.

Lower water

More of the gorge in view.

As the river drops, more rock and cliff face becomes visible. Seasonal island and pool activities may become possible, subject to the operator’s river assessment.

Lower water does not guarantee that every activity is operating.

Water levels follow conditions across the river’s catchment, not a fixed calendar. Check your priority activity for your dates and leave room to move weather-dependent outings.

02 · How long to stay

Give each day a little breathing room.

Three nights can work well for a first visit. This is an itinerary idea to adapt, rather than a fixed package.

Two nights

Prioritise the Falls and one other outing, shaped around your flights.

Three nights

Leave room for the Falls, a river outing and a wildlife or adventure day.

Four nights or more

Add a full-day trip, enjoy your lodge’s programme or slow the pace.

  1. Day 1

    Arrive and settle in.

    Allow for the transfer and check-in. If arrival time permits, consider a sunset cruise; otherwise keep the evening simple.

    Keep the arrival day flexible.
  2. Day 2

    The Falls, with an open afternoon.

    Make the rainforest walk the main event. Add a helicopter flight for another perspective, or return to your hotel.

    One main outing leaves space for a change of plan.
  3. Day 3

    Choose wildlife or adventure.

    A game drive leaves some free time. Rafting or a Chobe day trip calls for a full day. Choose one rather than trying to combine everything.

    Cross-border trips need extra planning.
  4. Day 4

    Breakfast and onward travel.

    Work backwards from your flight or onward transfer. Leave time for check-out, collection and any border formalities before committing to another timed activity.

    Share the exact flight details with your transfer provider.

05 · Arrivals & getting around

Join up the parts between outings.

Confirm the airport
Victoria Falls Airport is in Zimbabwe; Livingstone Airport is in Zambia. Give your transfer provider the airport, flight number and arrival time so the route is clear.
Plan the return journey
A town shuttle may not serve every restaurant or activity departure. Confirm collection points and the journey back, especially in the evening.
Check border arrangements
Chobe visits Botswana, while Livingstone Island is accessed from Zambia. Check entry and return requirements for your passport and itinerary before booking a cross-border outing.
Pack for your programme
Walking shoes, sun protection and a way to keep belongings dry are useful starting points. Use the operator’s specific packing list for rafting, gorge activities or a river outing.
Official entry information

Check the relevant authority for current requirements for your passport and route.

06 · Before you book

Make the quotation match the trip.

A few clear answers make it easier to compare your options.

The full total
Ask which meals, drinks, transfers, activities and mandatory park or conservation charges are included.
The exact booking
Confirm the room category or activity programme, departure point, duration and participation requirements.
Changes and cancellations
Check the booking terms and what happens if weather, water levels or your travel plans change.
